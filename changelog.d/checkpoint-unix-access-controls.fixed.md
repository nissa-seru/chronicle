- Atomic metadata replacement on Linux and macOS retains the checkpoint's owner, group, mode and per-file ACL, including inherited ACL entries. Replacement files stay private while their contents are written; failure to read or restore these controls leaves the previous checkpoint intact. macOS checkpoints with `com.apple.macl` authorization labels are rejected rather than losing that policy. Existing checkpoints must be readable regular files (symlink destinations are rejected), and the writer needs permission to restore their ownership and ACL.
- Linux checkpoints require readable POSIX ACL/security-xattr APIs and retain visible security-label xattrs and capabilities. Filesystems that report these APIs as unsupported are rejected on replacement. Staging files whose initial security labels differ from the destination, unfamiliar system ACL attributes, and content-bound IMA/EVM signatures are rejected instead of silently weakening policy. Path-based MAC policy must cover both final checkpoint names and `.chronicle-checkpoint-*` staging names; attributes hidden by the filesystem from the caller are outside the preservation contract.
